# Welcome to GDG @ Seneca: info session

Text draft of the deck. The "On screen" parts match what is on each slide. Speaker notes in the PowerPoint come from the Notes sections below, so edit them here.

Public listing: https://gdg.community.dev/events/details/google-gdg-on-campus-seneca-polytechnic-toronto-canada-presents-welcome-to-gdg-seneca-info-session/
Runtime: 60 minutes, including 10 minutes of check-in at the start and open networking at the end.

## What other chapters put in this session

I looked at how other GDG on Campus chapters run their info sessions before writing the outline. The pattern is consistent across chapters and across years.

Every session covers four things: what GDG on Campus is, who the core team is, what members get out of it, and what events are planned. Most add a short technical talk so people leave having learned something instead of only having heard a pitch. Most end with Q&A and unstructured time to talk.

Seneca's own February 2026 info session ran one hour online and used this order: meet the team, what is GDG, tech talks by the team, then networking. Ten people RSVP'd.

Two things other chapters do that we currently do not plan to:

The Solution Challenge gets its own slide, sometimes its own session. It is Google's annual global student competition. Chapters use it as the reason to join now rather than later, because teams have to form before the deadline.

Tech domain tracks get named explicitly. Chapters list the areas they will run events in, usually some mix of machine learning, web, Android, and cloud. Naming the tracks lets a student decide whether the chapter covers what they care about.

Both are worth adding. The tracks slide also answers a question we will get anyway.

Keep the demo to ten minutes. It is the block that always runs long.

This is a live demo, not a workshop: there is no time in ten minutes to walk the room through claiming GitHub Student Pack or JetBrains licenses. Decide the exact question or task to demo during rehearsal, not on stage.

Keep it to ten minutes and rehearse the whole thing once against the room's projector and the room's wifi. Have a recorded fallback ready, because campus wifi fails during live demos more often than it should.

End by pointing people at guides/ on the linktree for GitHub Student Pack, JetBrains, and the context template, so they can finish the setup themselves.
